# CROWN Transaction and Diligence Redlines

## Purpose

This document identifies corrections required before any transaction model, acquisition agreement, investor handout, or buyer presentation is treated as authoritative.

## 1. Continuity Term

**Required correction:** Replace any 36-month founder-employment or continuity term with the approved 24-month structure unless a later negotiated term supersedes it.

The 24-month structure should be divided into:

- Months 1-6: transfer, stabilization, release authority, operating handoff, and pilot readiness
- Months 7-12: commercialization, training, implementation support, and early adoption
- Months 13-24: scaling, relationship transfer, successor readiness, and founder-dependency reduction

## 2. Product Taxonomy

**Required correction:** Do not place admissions, billing, portals, scheduling, or other major operating functions inside the Core definition.

Use the following taxonomy consistently:

### Core

- Shared multi-tenant platform foundation
- Canonical student information and system-of-record functions
- Identity, permissions, tenant isolation, shared data, integrations, auditability, navigation, reporting, and workflow infrastructure

### Modules

- Admissions
- Enrollment and re-enrollment
- Billing, tuition, payments, and financial aid
- Attendance, discipline, gradebook, and student records
- Communications, portals, scheduling, and operations

### Add-ons

- CROWN Compass
- Board governance
- Professional development
- Spiritual life
- Service and outreach
- Advanced analytics and specialized integrations

## 3. Verified Facts Versus Modeled Assumptions

Every financial or commercial model should include a visible status label for each input:

- Verified actual
- Contracted
- Qualified pipeline
- Management estimate
- Scenario assumption
- Future target

The following must not be presented as verified without documentary support:

- Number of launch schools
- Average students per school
- Per-student or per-school pricing
- Payment volume
- Payment-processing residual percentage
- Financial-aid participation
- Conversion rate
- Revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, or valuation outcomes

## 4. Separation of Economics

The agreement and model should separately identify:

1. Acquisition price for acquired assets and intellectual property
2. Cash paid at closing
3. Seller note or deferred consideration
4. Founder employment compensation
5. Retention compensation for other transition personnel
6. Performance earnout
7. Optional Arete Advisory Group services
8. Payment-processing or other recurring-revenue participation

No component should be counted twice or used to obscure the value assigned to another component.

## 5. Release-Status Disclosure

Buyer materials should state that CROWN is a controlled release candidate and that production authorization is not complete until the governing release authority accepts all required evidence on the approved release identity.

Remaining proof areas should be disclosed accurately, including:

- Live authenticated browser and tenant evidence
- Rollback and restore
- External secrets-management operations
- Privacy and student-data controls
- Clean-room transferability
- Final same-release-identity authorization

## 6. Intellectual Property and Chain of Title

Diligence should include:

- Repository ownership and access records
- Commit and pull-request history
- Contributor agreements, assignments, contractor agreements, and employment terms
- Third-party code and license inventory
- Open-source obligations
- AI-assisted development disclosure and review controls
- Domain, brand, design, documentation, database, and deployment-asset ownership
- Evidence supporting any specific contributor attribution that must be disclosed

Active buyer and diligence materials should not enumerate early or interim collaborators unless identification is legally necessary, authorized by the Founder/Product Owner, and supported by durable evidence.

## 7. Arete Advisory Group Retained Rights

The transaction documents should clearly distinguish:

- Assets transferred with CROWN
- Pre-existing Arete Advisory Group methods, frameworks, consulting tools, templates, training materials, relationships, and know-how
- Any licensed rather than assigned assets
- Any post-closing services delivered under a separate agreement

The acquisition should not unintentionally transfer unrelated Arete intellectual property or business operations.

## 8. Privacy, Security, and Student Data

Diligence and definitive agreements should address:

- FERPA and applicable student-privacy responsibilities
- Data-processing roles and contractual allocation
- Security controls and incident response
- Tenant isolation
- Data retention, deletion, export, and migration
- Subprocessors and cloud services
- Authentication and privileged access
- Backup, restore, disaster recovery, and business continuity
- Cyber insurance and breach allocation

## 9. Payment and Financial-Service Boundaries

Any payment-processing economics must be contingent on:

- Written provider agreements
- Permitted revenue-sharing structure
- Compliance and underwriting approval
- School and parent disclosures
- Chargeback, fraud, refund, and reconciliation responsibilities
- Data-security allocation
- Termination and migration provisions

CompuWerx or any other processor should be presented as an integration or commercial relationship only to the extent supported by an executed agreement.

## 10. Clean-Room Transferability

A buyer should be able to operate the platform from documented assets without relying on undocumented founder knowledge.

Evidence should include:

- Reproducible environment setup
- Dependency and infrastructure inventory
- Architecture and data-flow documentation
- Deployment and rollback procedures
- Secrets and configuration inventory
- Operational runbooks
- Support and incident procedures
- Release governance
- Backup and restore proof
- New-operator validation

## 11. Agreement Protections

Definitive documents should address:

- Exact assets acquired and excluded
- Representations and warranties
- Disclosure schedules
- Indemnification caps, baskets, survival periods, and exclusions
- Earnout definitions, accounting rules, buyer operating covenants, and dispute procedures
- Employment duties, authority, compensation, benefits, termination, severance, and good-reason protections
- Noncompetition and nonsolicitation limits consistent with applicable law
- Transition milestones and acceptance criteria
- Treatment of product changes, shutdown, resale, or integration during the earnout

## 12. Immediate Redline Priorities

Before external circulation:

1. Correct the continuity period to 24 months.
2. Correct Core, Modules, and Add-ons definitions.
3. Relabel all modeled economics.
4. Separate purchase price, employment, retention, earnout, consulting, and payment revenue.
5. Insert the controlled-release-candidate disclosure.
6. Add IP chain-of-title and AI-assisted-development diligence.
7. Define retained Arete assets.
8. Add privacy, security, payment, and transferability schedules.
