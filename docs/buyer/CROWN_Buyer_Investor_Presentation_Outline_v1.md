# CROWN Buyer and Investor Presentation Outline

## Slide 1 — CROWN

**Christian School Management Solution**

A unified operating platform for Christian schools, combining student information, finance, admissions, communications, operations, governance, analytics, and mission-centered engagement.

Transaction concept: acquisition of the CROWN platform and intellectual property with a 24-month founder-led continuity and market-transition agreement.

## Slide 2 — The Problem

Christian schools commonly operate through fragmented combinations of student systems, billing tools, spreadsheets, communications products, manual workflows, and disconnected reporting.

This creates:

- Duplicate data and inconsistent records
- Poor visibility across departments
- Manual reconciliation and administrative burden
- Weak executive and board reporting
- Difficult parent, student, teacher, and staff experiences
- Limited mission-specific functionality

## Slide 3 — The CROWN Architecture

### Core

- Multi-tenant platform foundation
- Canonical student information and system-of-record truth
- Identity, roles, permissions, tenant isolation, auditability, and integrations
- Shared navigation, dashboards, reporting, and workflow services

### Modules

- Admissions and enrollment
- Tuition, billing, payments, and financial aid
- Attendance, discipline, gradebook, and student records
- Communications, portals, scheduling, and operations

### Add-ons

- CROWN Compass
- Board governance
- Professional development
- Spiritual life
- Service and outreach
- Advanced analytics and specialized integrations

## Slide 4 — Why CROWN Is Different

- Designed specifically for Christian-school operations and community needs
- Broad unified platform rather than disconnected point products
- Shared architecture with tenant isolation below the presentation layer
- Persona-specific dashboards and workflows
- Integrated governance, mission, and decision-support capabilities
- Founder familiarity with Christian-school leaders, conferences, and operating realities

## Slide 5 — What Exists Today

- Substantial platform, module, dashboard, workflow, test, and repository evidence
- Mature release-governance structure and extensive automated verification
- Multi-tenant product architecture and broad persona coverage
- Buyer-ready opportunity for technical diligence and controlled transition

**Disclosure:** CROWN remains a controlled release candidate. Final production authorization depends on completion of remaining live-runtime and operational evidence.

## Slide 6 — Market Entry and Adoption Strategy

### Phase 1: Transfer and Pilot Readiness

- Complete production authorization and operational transfer
- Prepare implementation, onboarding, support, pricing, and contracting
- Select pilot schools and buyer-channel partners

### Phase 2: Early Commercial Activation

- Launch controlled pilots
- Validate onboarding, support, retention, and product usage
- Refine packaging and integrations

### Phase 3: Scale

- Expand through buyer relationships, associations, conferences, and school networks
- Build repeatable implementation and customer-success operations
- Add strategic modules and add-ons based on verified demand

## Slide 7 — Strategic Buyer Fit

CROWN is most attractive to an organization seeking one or more of the following:

- Entry into Christian education technology
- Expansion of an existing faith-and-flourishing platform
- A school operating system that can support payments, services, data, and engagement
- A differentiated product for an established Christian-school distribution channel
- A founder-led market and product transition rather than a stand-alone code purchase

## Slide 8 — Revenue Architecture

Potential revenue components should be modeled separately:

- Core platform subscription
- Module subscriptions
- Add-on subscriptions
- Implementation and onboarding
- Training and professional services
- Payment-processing participation where contractually permitted
- Partner or channel arrangements

All school-count, student-count, pricing, payment-volume, conversion, and revenue figures remain assumptions until supported by contracts, pipeline evidence, or independently verified market data.

## Slide 9 — Transaction and Transition

Recommended transaction structure:

1. Acquisition price for platform and acquired intellectual property
2. Deferred or contingent acquisition consideration
3. 24-month founder and selected-team employment or retention package
4. Earnout tied to measurable outcomes
5. Separately documented ancillary revenue participation

The 24-month transition is designed to complete technical transfer, market activation, implementation readiness, relationship transfer, and buyer independence.

## Slide 10 — Decision and Next Steps

Proposed next-stage process:

- Execute mutual confidentiality agreement
- Deliver controlled management presentation and diligence index
- Conduct strategic-fit and transaction-structure discussion
- Open technical, commercial, legal, security, privacy, and financial diligence
- Agree on release-authority milestones and pilot-readiness criteria
- Develop indication of interest and transition term sheet

## Presentation Guardrails

- Use the public name **CROWN**, not Crown2026, in buyer-facing materials.
- Present the 24-month continuity term consistently.
- Do not treat modeled economics as actual performance.
- Do not represent production authorization as complete.
- Do not assign historical contribution credit without repository or documentary support.
- Do not enumerate early or interim collaborators unless identification is legally necessary, authorized by the Founder/Product Owner, and evidence-supported.
