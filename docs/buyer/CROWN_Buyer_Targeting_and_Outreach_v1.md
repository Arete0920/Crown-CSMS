# CROWN Buyer Targeting and Outreach Plan

## Objective

Identify and approach organizations capable of acquiring, funding, distributing, integrating, or accelerating CROWN while preserving its Christian-school market focus and supporting a 24-month founder-led transition.

## Targeting Principles

A qualified target should demonstrate several of the following:

- Clear alignment with Christian education, faith-and-flourishing, or mission-driven technology
- Ability to fund and operate a software acquisition
- Existing distribution to schools, churches, ministries, or Christian families
- Product, data, implementation, payments, or customer-success capabilities
- Willingness to preserve CROWN's market identity and mission fit
- Capacity to support production hardening and commercial scale

Targets should be classified by likely role rather than placed in one undifferentiated buyer list.

## Priority 1 — Strategic Acquirer

### Gloo

**Likely role:** Strategic acquirer or platform sponsor

**Why it may fit:**

- Faith-centered technology and engagement orientation
- Potential strategic value in school, family, church, data, and community connections
- Greater likelihood than a trade association of possessing acquisition, product, data, and growth capabilities
- Opportunity to position CROWN as a Christian-school operating layer within a broader faith-and-flourishing ecosystem

**Primary discussion:**

A confidential strategic-fit conversation focused on CROWN's product breadth, Christian-school specialization, data and engagement potential, current release position, and founder-led 24-month transfer.

**Evidence to prepare:**

- Architecture and platform map
- Release-readiness summary
- School-market problem statement
- Commercial model with assumptions clearly labeled
- Integration and ecosystem thesis
- IP and transition summary

## Priority 2 — Mission-Aligned Capital or Strategic Sponsor

### Herzog Foundation

**Likely role:** Mission-aligned investor, sponsor, commercialization partner, or joint-venture participant

**Why it may fit:**

- Strong alignment with Christian education and school growth
- Potential interest in infrastructure that strengthens school operations and sustainability
- Ability to support pilot access, validation, capital, and market credibility

**Primary discussion:**

Present CROWN as enabling infrastructure for stronger Christian-school operations, governance, finance, and leadership—not merely as a software product.

**Evidence to prepare:**

- Mission-impact framework
- School operating outcomes
- Pilot model
- Governance and measurement plan
- Capital requirements and use of funds
- Acquisition, investment, and partnership alternatives

## Priority 3 — Distribution and Market-Validation Partner

### Association of Christian Schools International

**Likely role:** Channel, validation, pilot, content, professional-development, or member-benefit partner

**Why it may fit:**

- Direct reach into Christian schools and school leaders
- Potential conference, content, research, training, and pilot opportunities
- Ability to validate buyer demand and create access to decision makers

**Constraint:**

Existing association partnerships with established education-technology or financial-service providers may limit acquisition or exclusivity. CROWN should therefore approach ACSI first as a strategic channel and validation relationship unless direct acquisition interest is explicitly established.

**Primary discussion:**

Explore controlled pilot access, member needs, conference visibility, research validation, professional-development alignment, and nonexclusive partnership structures.

## Priority 4 — Network and Pilot Partner

### Edify

**Likely role:** International Christian-school network, pilot, market-validation, or mission-impact partner

**Why it may fit:**

- Christian education mission alignment
- Potential access to school networks and operating contexts outside the conventional U.S. market
- Opportunity to validate lightweight, modular, multilingual, or resource-constrained deployment models

**Primary discussion:**

A pilot or strategic-learning relationship rather than an immediate acquisition assumption.

## Priority 5 — Commercial Integration Partner

### CompuWerx

**Likely role:** Payments, billing, or financial-services integration partner; possible strategic buyer only after diligence

**Why it may fit:**

- Potential relevance to tuition, payment processing, reconciliation, and school financial workflows
- Opportunity to create recurring payment-related value inside CROWN

**Constraint:**

No acquisition or revenue-sharing claim should be made without a written agreement, compliance review, underwriting approval, and clear allocation of fraud, chargeback, refund, reconciliation, data-security, and termination responsibilities.

## Outreach Sequence

### Stage 1 — Confidential Preparation

- Finalize acquisition brief and presentation
- Complete buyer-facing release disclosure
- Prepare transaction framework and diligence index
- Confirm intellectual-property and contributor records
- Separate verified facts from modeled assumptions

### Stage 2 — Warm Introduction Strategy

Use trusted relationships, Christian-school conferences, advisors, board members, ministry leaders, investors, and sector contacts to seek direct introductions to senior decision makers.

The first communication should request a confidential strategic discussion, not send the entire data room or lead with an unsupported valuation.

### Stage 3 — Initial Meeting

The initial meeting should cover:

- Strategic problem and product thesis
- Why CROWN fits the target
- Current product and release status
- Acquisition and 24-month transition concept
- Commercial assumptions and validation needs
- Decision process and next diligence step

### Stage 4 — Controlled Diligence

After confidentiality protections are in place, provide staged access to:

- Product and architecture materials
- Repository and release evidence
- Security, privacy, and operational evidence
- Commercial model and pipeline evidence
- Intellectual-property and contributor documentation
- Transaction and transition framework

## Initial Outreach Message

CROWN is a unified Christian school management platform developed to address the fragmented student, financial, operational, communications, governance, and mission-centered systems used by many Christian schools. We are evaluating a strategic acquisition or sponsorship relationship that would include the CROWN platform and intellectual property together with a 24-month founder-led transition to complete operational transfer and accelerate market adoption. We believe there may be a strong strategic fit with your work in Christian education and faith-centered technology, and we would welcome a confidential introductory discussion.

## Meeting Qualification Questions

- Does the organization have authority and capacity to acquire or sponsor a software platform?
- Is Christian-school technology part of its strategic agenda?
- What existing product, payments, data, association, or school relationships would affect fit?
- Would the organization preserve CROWN's Christian-school market positioning?
- Can it support product operations, implementation, customer success, and security?
- What evidence and milestones would be required before an indication of interest?
- Who controls the decision and diligence process?

## Current Recommendation

Use a role-based process:

1. Pursue Gloo as the leading strategic-acquirer conversation.
2. Pursue Herzog Foundation as a mission-aligned capital, sponsorship, or joint-venture conversation.
3. Pursue ACSI as a channel, validation, and pilot conversation.
4. Use Edify for network learning and pilot validation.
5. Evaluate CompuWerx through a payments and commercial-integration diligence track.

This ranking is a working strategy, not proof of buyer interest. No organization should be described as an interested buyer until direct evidence exists.
