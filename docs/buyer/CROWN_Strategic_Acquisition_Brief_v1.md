# CROWN Strategic Acquisition Brief

## Executive Summary

CROWN is a Christian school management platform designed to unify student information, admissions, enrollment, finance, communications, operations, governance, and school-community workflows within one multi-tenant system.

The proposed transaction is a strategic acquisition of the CROWN platform and related intellectual property, paired with a 24-month founder-led continuity and market-transition agreement. The purpose of the continuity period is to preserve product knowledge, complete operational transfer, support buyer integration, and accelerate adoption within the Christian school market.

CROWN should be presented as a controlled release candidate rather than as an already authorized production platform. Repository-level evidence is substantial, but final production authorization remains dependent on completion of live runtime, rollback, secrets-management, privacy, transferability, and same-release-identity evidence.

## Product Structure

### Core Platform

The Core is the shared technical and data foundation:

- Multi-tenant school architecture
- Canonical student information and system-of-record functions
- Identity, authentication, roles, permissions, and tenant isolation
- Shared navigation, dashboards, reporting, integrations, and workflow infrastructure
- Data governance, auditability, release controls, and operational administration

### Modules

Modules extend the Core with major school operating functions:

- Admissions and applicant management
- Enrollment and re-enrollment
- Tuition, billing, payments, and financial aid
- Attendance, discipline, gradebook, and student records
- Communications and stakeholder portals
- Scheduling, operations, and staff workflows
- Parent, student, teacher, administrator, executive, and board experiences

### Add-ons

Optional strategic capabilities include:

- CROWN Compass decision support
- Board governance and board-pack workflows
- Professional development hub
- Spiritual life and discipleship tools
- Service, outreach, and community-engagement functions
- Advanced analytics and specialized integrations

## Strategic Value

CROWN is differentiated by its Christian-school-specific design, integrated operating model, founder market familiarity, and broad product surface. It is intended to reduce fragmented systems and disconnected workflows while preserving the mission, governance, and community requirements of Christian schools.

The platform can provide value to a buyer through:

- Entry into or expansion within Christian education technology
- A unified product architecture rather than a collection of disconnected point solutions
- Cross-sell opportunities across core operations, finance, governance, analytics, and engagement
- Founder-led relationships, conference visibility, and market knowledge
- A structured path from controlled release candidate to scaled commercial operation

## Transaction Concept

The recommended structure separates five economic components so that each can be evaluated independently:

1. Purchase price for the CROWN platform and acquired intellectual property
2. Deferred or contingent acquisition consideration
3. Founder and selected-team employment or retention compensation during the 24-month transition
4. Performance-based earnout tied to verified commercial outcomes
5. Any payment-processing or ancillary recurring-revenue participation

This structure avoids blending acquisition value, salary, consulting, retention, and contingent revenue into one number.

## 24-Month Continuity Plan

### Months 1-6: Transfer and Stabilization

- Complete intellectual-property, repository, architecture, environment, and operational handoff
- Resolve remaining production-authorization evidence
- Establish buyer governance, product ownership, release authority, and support procedures
- Align branding, pricing, contracts, implementation, and customer-success processes
- Prepare pilot and early-adopter deployment plans

### Months 7-12: Commercial Activation

- Support pilots and initial implementations
- Train buyer product, engineering, implementation, sales, and support personnel
- Refine packaging, onboarding, and buyer-channel strategy
- Complete priority integrations and production-hardening work

### Months 13-24: Scale and Independence

- Expand adoption through buyer channels and Christian-school relationships
- Transfer remaining founder-dependent knowledge and relationships
- Establish repeatable implementation, support, and release operations
- Complete final transition milestones and successor leadership readiness

## Current Release Position

CROWN has meaningful repository, test, workflow, architecture, and certification evidence. However, final production authorization should not be represented as complete until the controlling release authority confirms all required evidence on the same approved release identity.

The remaining proof families include:

- Live authenticated browser and tenant evidence
- Rollback and restore evidence
- External secrets-management operations
- Privacy and student-data controls
- Clean-room transferability
- Final same-release-identity authorization

## Diligence Principles

Buyer-facing materials should consistently distinguish among:

- Verified product and repository facts
- Current release status
- Modeled commercial assumptions
- Proposed transaction terms
- Future roadmap items

No modeled school counts, student counts, pricing, payment volume, conversion rate, revenue, or growth assumptions should be presented as contracted, realized, or independently verified unless supporting evidence is included.

## Recommended Next Step

Use this brief as the foundation for a confidential management presentation, a buyer-specific strategic-fit summary, and a controlled diligence data room. Initial discussions should focus on strategic fit, transaction structure, continuity, release status, and the evidence required for a buyer to advance to technical and commercial diligence.
