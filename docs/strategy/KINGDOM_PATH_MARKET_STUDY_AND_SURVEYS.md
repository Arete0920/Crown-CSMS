# Kingdom Path Strategic Market Study and Survey Intelligence

**Status:** Product and engineering contract

## Purpose

Kingdom Path uses verified demographic, economic, educational, faith-community, competitive, operational, and survey evidence to support school strategy. It is not a marketing-only report.

The study must inform:

- tuition positioning;
- affordability analysis;
- financial-aid planning;
- enrollment goals;
- program development;
- marketing priorities;
- transportation opportunities;
- geographic and facility expansion.

## Strategic market study layers

The demographic preparation workflow captures ten required sections:

1. school profile;
2. geography;
3. economics;
4. student market;
5. competition;
6. faith/community;
7. enrollment performance;
8. financial profile;
9. program/capacity;
10. strategic objectives.

A study cannot be committed until all ten sections are present.

Every external dataset must be represented in `source_provenance`. Crown must not present estimated demographic, competitor, advertising, or affordability figures as verified live truth without a traceable source.

## Decision uses

### Tuition and affordability

Market economics should help leadership understand affordability bands, price pressure, competitive positioning, and likely full-pay/partial-aid demand. Demographics inform pricing decisions; they do not mechanically set tuition.

### Financial aid

Market evidence may inform expected aid demand, aid-budget planning, and grade-level affordability pressure. Individual awards remain governed by the existing aid/Jireh domain and its policies.

### Enrollment goals

Enrollment goals should consider addressable population, private-school participation, mission fit, geography, conversion history, current enrollment, and verified grade capacity.

### Programs

Age cohorts, working-parent patterns, homeschool presence, feeder relationships, geographic concentration, and survey demand may support evaluation of preschool/daycare, extended care, transportation, Home Academy, dual enrollment, athletics, summer programs, and other offerings.

### Expansion

Kingdom Path should compare lower-capital responses such as transportation, feeder/church partnerships, program expansion, or digital/homeschool offerings before recommending physical campus expansion.

## Survey intelligence

The Survey / Sentiment Engine provides seven standard enrollment-oriented survey types:

- Inquiry;
- Post-Tour;
- New Family;
- Parent Pulse;
- Re-enrollment Intent;
- Lost Prospect;
- Exit.

Survey evidence helps explain **why** families behave as they do, while demographics describe who is in the market and Admissions/CRM records what they actually did.

### Retention

Parent Pulse, re-enrollment intent, and exit surveys may surface:

- satisfaction;
- mission perception;
- academic concerns;
- communication concerns;
- tuition pressure;
- aid need;
- likely re-enrollment intention;
- avoidable withdrawal reasons;
- competitor destinations.

These are decision-support indicators. Crown must describe them as signals requiring attention, not deterministic predictions that a family will leave.

### Growth

Inquiry, Post-Tour, New Family, and Lost Prospect surveys may surface:

- first awareness source;
- decisive enrollment factor;
- Portrait outcomes valued by families;
- barriers to conversion;
- affordability concerns;
- admissions experience;
- competitors considered.

These insights should inform campaign targeting, source attribution, messaging, program priorities, and affordability strategy.

## Integrated evidence model

**Demographics** answer: Who could enroll?

**Surveys** answer: Why would they enroll, stay, or leave?

**CRM / Marketing** answers: How did we engage them?

**Admissions** answers: What did they actually do?

**Jireh / Aid** answers: Where did affordability affect access?

**Enrollment / Capacity** answers: Where do we have room and what did we fill?

Together, these inputs support tuition, aid, enrollment, program, and expansion decisions without duplicating canonical system authority.
