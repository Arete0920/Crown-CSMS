# CROWN Student Privacy and Data Protection Program

**Status:** Working compliance-control specification

## Privacy principles

1. School/customer control over education data.
2. Purpose limitation to contracted educational and school-operational services.
3. No sale of student personal information.
4. No behaviorally targeted advertising using student data.
5. No unrelated commercial use of school-authorized child data.
6. Data minimization.
7. Least-privilege access.
8. Tenant isolation.
9. Transparent subprocessors.
10. Defined retention and deletion.
11. Parent/student access supported through school-controlled workflows.
12. Security incidents handled through documented response and notification procedures.

## FERPA-aligned vendor controls

For customers and records where FERPA applies, CROWN contracts and operations should support:

- services that the school would otherwise perform itself or contract for;
- school control over the use and maintenance of education records;
- use only for the purpose for which the disclosure was made;
- restrictions on redisclosure;
- access limited to legitimate educational/operational purposes;
- return or deletion at contract termination as agreed;
- documented subprocessors and equivalent restrictions.

## COPPA controls

For users under 13:

- school authorization may be used only within the educational context and for the school's benefit;
- CROWN must provide the school with notice of collection, use, and disclosure practices;
- unrelated commercial use requires a separate lawful consent basis;
- direct-to-child signup should be blocked unless the approved consent workflow supports it;
- school-authorized child accounts must be linked to an authorized school/tenant;
- data collected from children must be limited to what the service needs.

## PPRA support

Where PPRA applies to the school/customer:

- identify surveys that touch protected categories;
- permit school control of parental notice/inspection/consent/opt-out requirements;
- restrict access to sensitive survey responses;
- prevent marketing use of protected survey data;
- log administrative access and exports.

## Retention and deletion

Every data domain must have:

- business purpose;
- data owner;
- minimum retention requirement;
- maximum retention target;
- deletion/archival trigger;
- legal-hold exception;
- backup-treatment rule;
- deletion evidence.

## Sensitive domains requiring enhanced controls

- student health/nurse data;
- disability/accommodation information;
- counseling/pastoral care;
- discipline;
- financial aid;
- household financial information;
- identity documents;
- emergency/safety information;
- prayer/pastoral notes where maintained;
- authentication/security data.

## Support access

Support personnel access to customer data should be:

- role-controlled;
- time-bounded when feasible;
- purpose documented;
- logged;
- limited to the minimum tenant/data required;
- reviewed for sensitive domains.

## Subprocessors

Maintain an approved subprocessor register with:

- vendor and service;
- purpose;
- categories of data;
- hosting/processing location where known;
- security/compliance evidence;
- contractual privacy/security terms;
- incident-notification obligations;
- deletion/return terms;
- review date and owner.

## State-law overlay

Before national rollout, maintain a state requirements matrix and approved contract addenda. Do not assume that FERPA/COPPA controls alone satisfy state student-privacy laws.
