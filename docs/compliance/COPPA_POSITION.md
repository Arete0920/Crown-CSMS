# CROWN COPPA Position and Readiness Procedure

**Status:** DOCUMENTED PROCEDURE / LEGAL REVIEW AND OPERATIONAL VERIFICATION OPEN  
**Prepared:** 2026-10-07  
**Accountable role:** Founder/Product Owner with qualified privacy review

## Scope and applicability

Evaluate each website, mobile application, portal, account flow, and enabled integration against COPPA's child-directed and actual-knowledge conditions. Include identifiers, device tracking, photos/voice, messages, and other covered information; do not assess only registration fields. A parent-operated experience and a direct child experience may have different collection facts. School affiliation or private-school status does not by itself exempt CROWN as an operator.

Use the amended rule and current FTC guidance, not solely an older checklist. This record does not assert COPPA certification or completed CROWN-wide compliance.

## Required decision before collection

For each experience, record intended users, age handling, collection sources/categories, purpose, recipients, tracking/SDKs, retention, rights handling, operator role, applicable authorization/consent basis, reviewer, and approval. Do not enable relevant collection until the decision and supporting workflow are verified.

Where school authorization is permitted, collection/use must remain within the educational context for the school's use and benefit, without unrelated commercial use. Verify the school's authorized representative and retain authorization plus the required notice. CROWN retains its operator obligations; contracts must not state that the school alone is responsible for CROWN's COPPA compliance.

Where verifiable parental consent is required, document the selected permitted verification method, notice version, consenting person's authority, permitted purposes/disclosures, timestamp, revocation, and evidence. Assess separately whether a third-party disclosure requires separate consent under the amended rule. Do not assume broad school approval authorizes every integration.

## Notice, rights, and minimization

Publish and deliver the applicable collection/use/disclosure notices. Maintain verified review, correction where applicable, deletion, consent revocation, and further-collection restriction procedures. Route school-context requests through authorized school personnel while preserving applicable parent rights.

Collect only information reasonably necessary for the approved activity. Prohibit sale, behavioral advertising, and unrelated profiling of school-authorized child data. Review analytics, diagnostics, support attachments, and external guidance for indirect collection/disclosure. An adult-role restriction on one feature does not resolve the entire service's child-privacy obligations.

## Security, vendors, and retention

Maintain a written security program with accountable ownership, risk assessment, safeguards, testing/review, and service-provider oversight as required. Verify actual recipients' contractual and security protections. Maintain written purpose-linked retention/deletion rules; do not retain children's information indefinitely. Reconcile backups, legal obligations, holds, and restored-data deletion handling with qualified review.

The [Security Operating Policy](SECURITY_OPERATING_POLICY.md), [Student Privacy Program](STUDENT_PRIVACY_DATA_PROTECTION_PROGRAM.md), [Retention Policy](RETENTION_POLICY.md), and [Subprocessor Register](SUBPROCESSOR_REGISTER.md) define the required control work; document existence alone is not proof of operation.

## Verification and closure

Test, for every applicable experience: unauthorized signup/collection is denied; school/parent authority is checked; the approved notice/consent is recorded; prohibited disclosure is blocked; revocation/review/deletion are effective; retention is bounded; vendors and tracking match disclosed practices; evidence excludes unnecessary personal information.

Operational completion requires approved applicability decisions, notices, verified authorization/consent paths, rights/retention tests, recipient reviews, security-program evidence, and qualified review of the intended release/customer scope. A passing form test or documented policy alone does not establish compliance.
